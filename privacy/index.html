Privacy Policy

Last updated 14 October 2026

This policy explains what the Wakey app keeps on your iPhone, what leaves it and why, who it is shared with, and how to delete all of it. Three things shape everything below: we never sell, license or share your personal information for anyone else's purposes, there is no advertising and no cross-app tracking, and the app measures how it is used only while the switch in Settings is on — Usage analytics says exactly what that involves.

About this policy

It covers the Wakey iPhone app and this website (together, the "App"). Wakey is made and run by an independent individual developer, not a company. Who is responsible for your data, and how to reach them, is set out under Contact.

"Personal information" means information relating to an identified or identifiable person, as defined by Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and British Columbia's Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA), the EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), the UK Data Protection Act 2018, the California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA/CPRA), the Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act (VCDPA) and other applicable privacy laws. Which parts of this policy apply to you depends on where you live; the sections on your rights say so explicitly.

Wakey is a convenience, not a safety device. Do not rely on it as your only alarm for anything critical. Whether an alarm rings depends on your iPhone: the alarm permission, the ringer volume, the Focus settings you chose, and the phone being on and charged.

The camera mission ("Hunt an object (AI)")

When you play this mission, single camera frames (about one every half second while the mission runs) are sent to our server and on to OpenAI's API, which answers whether the asked-for object, hand, face or gesture is in view. Because some prompts ask for a selfie, a frame can show your face.

  • Frames are not stored and not logged by Wakey; they are used only to make the decision and then discarded. OpenAI handles them under its API data usage policy.
  • No face recognition is performed, no biometric identifier is created, and nothing is matched against who you are.
  • The random install identifier and a per-day counter limit how many checks one phone can run. They carry no personal information.
  • The camera is used only during that mission and only with the camera permission you grant iOS. This mission is part of Wakey Pro.

Purchases

Wakey Pro is sold through the App Store and billed to your Apple ID. We never see your payment details. RevenueCat processes the purchase receipt so the app knows whether Pro is active; it holds an app user identifier (random while you are signed out, your account identifier when you are signed in), the receipt, and device information such as model, operating system and locale. Superwall shows the Wakey Pro screen and receives the same app user identifier and device information to decide which screen to show. Apple's own terms apply to the purchase itself.

Usage analytics

To see which parts of Wakey work and which do not, the App can record what people do in it, using PostHog as the analytics provider. The switch is in Settings → Privacy → "Share anonymous usage analytics." When it is off, nothing is sent.

What analytics records

  • alarm_created: which mission types the new alarm has and how many
  • wake_completed: the mission types, how many times you pressed Stop, and how many missions were swapped through the safety valve
  • signed_in (with the provider, Apple or Google), signed_out, account_deleted
  • Standard app events (installed, updated, opened, backgrounded), your device model, operating system version and language
  • A random identifier while you are signed out, and your account identifier when you are signed in, so one person's events group together
  • Your IP address reaches PostHog with each event, as with any web request. We set PostHog to discard it and not to derive a location from it, so analytics holds no location data.

What analytics never records

Alarm labels, alarm times, your name, your email address, camera frames, anything you type in a mission. Analytics sees that a wake was completed, never what the alarm was called or when it rang.

Server logs

Our hosting provider, Supabase, records standard server logs — IP address, timestamps and the request made — as a normal part of running a service and for security. We do not use them to profile you.

What we do not collect

We do not collect your location, contacts, photos library, health data, calendar or advertising identifier. We show no ads, build no advertising profiles and do not track you across other apps or websites. This website sets no cookies and has no tracking pixels, which is why you were not asked to consent to any.

Where your information is held, and international transfers

Supabase hosts our data in the United States (Oregon). RevenueCat, Superwall, OpenAI and PostHog also process data in the United States. If you are in the EU or the UK, these transfers rely on the providers' standard contractual clauses.

How long we keep it

  • Account data (alarms and wake history under your account): until you delete the account. It is then removed immediately; copies in our hosting provider's backups age out on its backup schedule (currently days, never more than a few weeks).
  • Camera frames: not kept at all.
  • Daily check counters: counts tied to the random install identifier, with no personal information.
  • Purchase records: as long as Apple and RevenueCat need them to honour your subscription.
  • Usage events: for as long as PostHog retains them under our plan. Deleting your account removes the link between you and the identifier they were grouped under; events already sent stay in PostHog pseudonymously unless you ask us to delete that person record too.
  • Server logs: for as long as our hosting provider retains them under our plan (days to a few weeks).

Deleting your account

In the App: Settings → Account → Delete account, then confirm. Deletion is immediate and permanent. It removes the account and the alarms and wake history stored under it, and the App starts a fresh analytics identifier. It does not remove the alarms on your iPhone (they keep ringing, as for someone who never signed in) or your purchase with Apple, which you can bring back any time with Restore Purchases. Events already sent to analytics stay pseudonymous under the old identifier; contact us to have that person record deleted as well. Deleting the App from your iPhone does not delete the account.

Security

Everything between the App and our providers travels encrypted. The App keeps your session in the iOS keychain. Access to our servers is limited to the developer.

Children

Wakey is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, contact us and we will delete it.

Your rights

Canada

Under PIPEDA and BC's PIPA you can ask what personal information we hold about you, ask for it to be corrected, and withdraw your consent. Deleting your account withdraws consent for everything held under it. You can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada or the Office of the Information and Privacy Commissioner for British Columbia.

European Union and United Kingdom

Under the GDPR and UK GDPR you have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. You can complain to your supervisory authority. Deleting your account in the App erases everything held under it; contact us for the analytics person record.

United States

Under the CCPA/CPRA, the VCDPA and similar state laws you can know what we collect, delete it, correct it, and opt out of sale or sharing — we do not sell or share personal information for advertising. We will not discriminate against you for exercising these rights.

To exercise any of these rights, delete your account in the App or contact us.
